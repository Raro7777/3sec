# Classic raising sims and relationship-progression games: how a stat loop becomes a story

Scope: Jikkyou Powerful Pro Yakyuu サクセス (console and パワプロアプリ), Princess Maker, Tokimeki Memorial, Persona Social Links/Confidants, Fire Emblem supports, plus storylet / quality-based narrative theory (Emily Short, Failbetter). The aim is transferable mechanics for a ~10-minute mobile volleyball card-raising run made by one developer.
Research date: 2026-10-21. Most sources are Wikipedia, press, and fan wikis. Primary developer interviews were hard to reach (see Gaps).

## 1. Power Pro サクセス: run structure, event characters, matches, failure, and its link to Uma Musume

### Takeaway
Success turns training into a story in three ways. The run takes place inside a fixed, authored life arc (high school leading to Koshien and the draft). Growth comes as experience points you spend yourself, not as automatic stat gains. Most character content comes from a modular "event deck" of collectible characters whose events also pay out stats. Failure states (injury, career-ending injury, not being drafted) are part of the drama. Staff openly said they modeled it on Tokimeki Memorial. Uma Musume is widely seen as a descendant: Konami sued Cygames over 18 training-system patents in 2023, and the case settled in 2025.

### Cited Findings
**Origin and lineage**
- The mode first appeared in 『実況パワフルプロ野球3』 (1996) and reached its 30th anniversary in 2026 — [ja.wikipedia サクセスモード](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/サクセスモード); [Famitsu 2026](https://www.famitsu.com/article/202608/79181)
- The developers have said they were "very conscious of" 『ときめきメモリアル』 when designing the mode, and that game is cited as the trigger for creating it — [ja.wikipedia サクセスモード](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/サクセスモード)
- Konami considered a plain player-edit mode and rejected it because "if you can just set every parameter to MAX it ends there and it's boring" (パラメータをオールMAXにして終わってしまうからつまらない). The constraint is what creates the game — [ja.wikipedia サクセスモード](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/サクセスモード)
- Japanese Wikipedia lists ウマ娘 プリティーダービー, 新日本プロレス STRONG SPIRITS and ブルーロック Project: World Champion as mobile apps with systems "very similar" to Success — [ja.wikipedia サクセスモード](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/サクセスモード)
- Konami sued Cygames in Tokyo District Court (disclosed 2023-05-17), seeking ¥4 billion in damages and an injunction over parts of Uma Musume's game system and program. Cygames filed invalidation trials against all 18 patents. The case settled on 2025-11-07 under confidential terms. Users had long noted that Uma Musume's training resembled Power Pro — [Automaton](https://automaton-media.com/articles/newsjp/20230517-247735/); [GameBusiness.jp 2025-11-08](https://www.gamebusiness.jp/article/2025/11/08/25448.html); [KAI-YOU](https://kai-you.net/article/93820)

**Run structure (console)**
- Early games ran "one-week turns over 3 years". More recent entries usually cover one year — [ja.wikipedia](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/サクセスモード)
- Training does not raise stats directly. It earns 経験点 (experience points), which the player spends on abilities afterwards — [ja.wikipedia](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/サクセスモード)
- Training costs stamina. Low stamina raises injury chance. A serious injury drops abilities sharply, and 再起不能 (career-ending injury) is a game over — [ja.wikipedia](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/サクセスモード)
- Motivation (やる気) has 5 levels, from 絶好調 down to 絶不調, and affects match performance and experience-point gain — [ja.wikipedia](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/サクセスモード)
- In matches you control only part of the game: as a batter, your own at-bats. As a pitcher, usually only the moments with runners on 2nd or 3rd — [ja.wikipedia](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/サクセスモード). The app version runs games automatically and hands you the key moments (試合は自動で進み、重要な場面は自分で操作) — [Konami official guide](https://www.konami.com/pawa/app/guide.html)
- Game-over or failure conditions include going undrafted (ドラフト不指名), being released, a "bomb" exploding into a career-ending injury, the team being disbanded, a tournament loss, and failing a baseball exam — [ja.wikipedia](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/サクセスモード)
- The girlfriend (彼女) system became a full feature in パワプロ5. Phoning her restores motivation and stamina. Money arrived in パワプロ6. 継承選手 (inherited players: your past Success creations appear as teammates) began in パワプロ9 — [ja.wikipedia](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/サクセスモード)
- Scenarios are grouped by intended difficulty and flavor: tutorial (パワフル〜), expert powerhouse schools (あかつき〜, 帝王〜, where you must keep passing practical exams), variety (high-randomness events), variety+expert, and match-only (few practices, experience earned in games) — [ja.wikipedia](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/サクセスモード)

**パワプロアプリ (mobile, released 2014-12-23)**
- The official guide states the goal as following a high-school-baseball story toward a Koshien win. Experience comes from practice and leisure actions, and earning as much as possible before the story ends is the key — [Konami guide](https://www.konami.com/pawa/app/guide.html)
- Event characters (イベキャラ) in your deck appear during Success and trigger events that grant experience. Higher rarity and level give more experience, and levels rise through enhancement — [Konami guide](https://www.konami.com/pawa/app/guide.html)
- As of 2018 there were over 300 event characters, organized into a deck by position (pitcher, catcher, infield, outfield). Some female characters become girlfriends once their rating is high enough, and girlfriend events and dates give large experience gains. Seasonal dates such as Christmas and New Year pay off big, so the relationship has to be prepared in advance — [4Gamer 2018-08-07](https://www.4gamer.net/games/278/G027881/20180807001) (a fan writer, not the developer)
- The same article lists about 20 live scenarios with very different genres, all built on one training core: Vampire High (drain blood for stamina and EXP), ダン＆ジョン高校 (RPG with weapon recipes and bosses, little baseball), 円卓高校 (Arthurian Holy Grail mystery), あかつき大附属 (beat the "Akatsuki Ten" to earn a starting spot; every regular is an event character), and 恵比留高校 (dark tone, added Aug 2018) — [4Gamer 2018](https://www.4gamer.net/games/278/G027881/20180807001)
- Risk events: "Dr. Daijoubu" offers a modification surgery. Success boosts stats greatly and failure cuts them greatly. The writer advises refusing it when a run is going well — [4Gamer 2018](https://www.4gamer.net/games/278/G027881/20180807001)
- Story flow: reach Koshien, then get drafted after graduation through scout evaluation. Finished players feed the online "Stadium" mode, so creating players and competing form one loop — [4Gamer 2018](https://www.4gamer.net/games/278/G027881/20180807001)
- App-only changes: the event deck, dating two or more girlfriends at once, and failed runs registering the player with random red (negative) special abilities. A failed run still produces a usable but flawed player instead of nothing — [ja.wikipedia](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/サクセスモード)
- To deepen a friendship with a teammate you have to raise their 評価 (rating) before 友情タッグ (friendship tag training) unlocks. One reviewer calls predicting next week's fixed events and deciding whether to rest or train the most enjoyable part, says event timing has a "gambling" quality, and says that in the end "real luck is needed". The review covers the 2026 spin-off パワプロアドベンチャーズ, which uses the same system — [Inside Games 2026-05-04](https://www.inside-games.jp/article/2026/05/04/180880.html)
- The same reviewer describes the "tremendous sense of achievement and release" of spending a stockpile of experience at once. Their criticism: SR cards are so strong that PR/R cards are hard to use — [Inside Games 2026-05-04](https://www.inside-games.jp/article/2026/05/04/180880.html)

### Inferences
- **The authored arc is the frame, and the stat loop is the content.** Each scenario fixes the start (enter school), midpoints (tournaments, exams), and climax (Koshien, draft). The weekly loop fills the space between, so every run feels like a story without any branching plot.
- **Delayed experience spending** ("bank then spend") creates a small payoff moment that automatic stat gains would not. A 10-minute run could bank points during the season and spend them in a "growth reveal" before the climactic match.
- **Event-deck cards do double duty.** One card is a character, a source of story beats, and a stat buff, so collecting cards is the same act as collecting story. This is the most transferable piece for a card-raising volleyball game.
- **Timing-gated relationship payoffs** (Christmas dates need prior affection) turn relationship-building into planning, which ties feeling to strategy.
- **Failure is graded, not binary.** Injury, red abilities, and going undrafted give a bad run a story ("the year she got hurt") while still producing a player.
- **Scenario variety on one core** (vampires, Arthur, RPG) is how a long-running service adds novelty cheaply. The training engine stays and only the event set and gimmick change.

### Gaps
- No primary Konami staff interview was retrieved (famitsu/4gamer developer interviews were not found in search). Typical run length for the app (often said to be about 15–30 minutes) could not be confirmed from a source.
- The detailed rules of the 爆弾 (bomb) injury-risk mechanic in Success, and specific teammate or rival story arcs (for example the パワプロクンポケット series' known dark stories), were not sourced.

## 2. Princess Maker: schedule raising, multiple endings, father-daughter attachment

### Takeaway
Princess Maker 2 runs a fixed eight-year (age 10 to 18) three-slots-per-month schedule, uses many interacting stats, and pays off with a large ending set (74) that includes a retrospective of her upbringing. The player is cast as a father, so the stat sheet reads as a person you are responsible for. Akai's stated design fix for the original's repetitiveness was "more character reactions and events and multiple paths to success."

### Cited Findings
- Akai's interest in the unit-training mechanics of Nobunaga's Ambition was one source of the first game, per a 2012 interview with co-creator Toshio Okada. The working title was "My Fair Child". To make it approachable to a male audience, the player is a father figure who adopts a girl — [Wikipedia: Princess Maker (video game)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princess_Maker_(video_game))
- PM2: you raise the daughter from age 10 to 18 (8 in-game years). Each month has three periods, each assigned an activity from training (academic, artistic, religious, military, magic), part-time jobs (unlocking with age, with promotions for repeated success), adventure, or free time. Income is limited each year — [Wikipedia: Princess Maker 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princess_Maker_2)
- Stress and rest must be managed to avoid illness, which costs money to treat. Diet changes body type, and excessive dieting is a health risk. Her astrological sign sets her guardian spirit and subtly shapes personality — [Wikipedia: PM2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princess_Maker_2)
- Periodic tests: October harvest festival contests (combat, dance, art, cooking). Rival girls appear at workplaces and contests and age alongside her. Losing an adventure battle does not kill her. Vacations are taken with the father, and the type depends on season — [Wikipedia: PM2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princess_Maker_2)
- 74 endings. At 18 she chooses a career, which is scored (queen or general high, barmaid low), and endings include a retrospective of her upbringing before the final evaluation — [Wikipedia: PM2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princess_Maker_2)
- Akai said the sequel aimed to answer complaints that the original felt repetitive, by adding more character reactions and events and multiple paths to success — [Wikipedia: PM2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princess_Maker_2)
- Akai (Wired, 1994) called it "a long-term game where you make a commitment and create your own world", in contrast to score-driven shooters. He also said it let players do what they can't in real life, since fathers were not expected to manage their daughters' lives — [Wikipedia: PM2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princess_Maker_2)
- Reception: reviewers praised the daughter's emotional depth and said raising her was more enjoyable than chasing elite endings. The training-and-rest cycle was criticized as repetitive. Some critics called parts of the content sexist, and a women's studies professor compared some aspects to incest — [Wikipedia: PM2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princess_Maker_2)
- Sales: about 30,000 copies by September 1993. Comptiq ranked it the most popular computer game of its month in January 1994 — [Wikipedia: PM2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princess_Maker_2)
- Princess Maker 2 Regeneration (2024–25) has graphics newly redrawn by Akai — [Gamespress](https://gamespress.com/Launch-Trailer-Released-for-Princess-Maker-2-Regeneration)

### Inferences
- **Attachment comes from responsibility plus visible change over time.** The portrait ages, the body changes, and rivals age with her. The ending retrospective replays the whole run, which turns many small schedule choices into a single life story. A volleyball run could end with a "season recap" montage of the girl's key moments, not just a stat card.
- **Many endings reward experimenting, not just optimizing.** Because low-prestige endings are still authored, a "bad" run produces something to see, and reviewers explicitly enjoyed the raising more than chasing elite endings.
- **The fix for repetition was reaction density, not system complexity.** Akai's answer was more character reactions and events. For a solo developer, short reaction lines for each activity and stat threshold are cheaper than new systems.
- **Caution on framing.** The father-daughter frame drew criticism of sexism and incest-like aspects. For a game about adult or young-adult female athletes, a coach or manager frame needs care to avoid the same reading.

### Gaps
- Whether PM2 tracks an explicit numeric "relationship with father" value, and exactly how endings are weighted (stat formula), is not covered in the retrieved sources. Fan wikis would confirm this. It is not asserted here.
- No direct Akai interview text in Japanese was retrieved.

## 3. Tokimeki Memorial: stats and relationships interlocked, the bomb mechanic, the confession ending

### Takeaway
Tokimeki Memorial makes self-improvement stats the currency of romance. Each heroine reacts to particular stats, and the main heroine demands balanced growth. The "bomb" is a neglect mechanic that punishes ignoring anyone and can chain. The single climactic payoff is a confession under the legendary tree at graduation. Its week-based three-year school calendar was the direct template for Power Pro Success.

### Cited Findings
- The player is a male student at Kirameki High who raises abilities through study, sports, and clubs while dating heroines. The goal is a confession under the legendary tree on graduation day. The legend says couples formed by a girl's confession there are happy forever — [ja.wikipedia ときめきメモリアル](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/ときめきメモリアル)
- Most heroines react to one particular parameter of the protagonist. 藤崎詩織 (Shiori) ignores you unless you grew in a balanced way, and her high demands earned her the nickname 「ラスボス」 (final boss) — [ja.wikipedia](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/ときめきメモリアル)
- The design started from re-living three years of school life, expressed one week at a time. Almost everything except dates happens on campus — [ja.wikipedia](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/ときめきメモリアル)
- The school-festival event branches on club activities and relationships into 1,497 or more theoretical patterns. It makes up about 40% of the game's entire message volume — [ja.wikipedia](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/ときめきメモリアル)
- It introduced "bust-up animation" (eyes and mouth move with expressions), with 5 expressions per portrait — [ja.wikipedia](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/ときめきメモリアル)
- 1.1 million copies sold by 1996. Word of mouth spread on PC networks — [ja.wikipedia](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/ときめきメモリアル)
- Series-level description: limited time split between pursuing classmates and raising school and sports abilities to make the protagonist attractive. Dates are frequent but brief, usually one multiple-choice question that moves the love meter. At the end, the character with the highest love meter confesses. A run takes about 5–10 hours — [en.wikipedia Tokimeki Memorial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokimeki_Memorial)
- Bomb (爆弾): a heroine's 傷心 (heartbreak) builds when you, for example, leave school alone after meeting her ("じゃお先に"), or turn down her date because you want to date someone else. When a bomb explodes, other heroines also get hurt, so bombs can chain and in the worst case your reputation collapses. The standard counter is to keep up moderate contact with the other girls too — [Game Watch 30th-anniversary retrospective](https://game.watch.impress.co.jp/docs/kikaku/1593642.html) (summarized via search; exact thresholds not confirmed)

### Inferences
- **Stats as the language of attraction**: raising stats is not separate from the relationship. It is how you court. For volleyball, a teammate who respects blocking, or a rival who only acknowledges you once your serve passes a threshold, makes stat goals personal.
- **A balanced-stats "final boss" relationship** gives the optimizer a reason to diversify instead of dumping everything into one stat.
- **Bombs are relationship upkeep cost.** They make neglect visible and create emergent drama ("I forgot about her and everything blew up"). In a 10-minute run, a lightweight version (a teammate feels sidelined if never picked for tag training, which lowers her morale) could add tension without a large text cost.
- **One event can carry huge combinatorial content.** The festival held about 40% of all text. Concentrating branching into a few climactic set-pieces, rather than spreading it evenly, maximizes perceived reactivity.
- **The climax is a single authored scene gated by accumulated state**, which is the same pattern as Power Pro's draft or Koshien.

### Gaps
- Exact bomb thresholds and decay rules, and the requirements for each heroine, were not confirmed from a primary or wiki source.
- No Konami developer interview on Tokimeki's design was retrieved.

## 4. Persona Social Links / Confidants, and Fire Emblem supports

### Takeaway
Persona 3 tied relationships to combat power. Each Social Link is an Arcana, and its rank multiplies the experience of Personas fused in that Arcana. Max rank unlocks that Arcana's ultimate Persona. A day/night calendar with scarce time slots makes every hangout a strategic trade-off, and links can reverse or break. Fire Emblem supports tie relationship to positioning in battle (adjacency builds points, and ranks C/B/A/S unlock conversations and stat bonuses).

### Cited Findings
- P3 Social Links are bonds with characters, each tied to a Major Arcana, and their rank rises by spending time with the character. Fusing a Persona of that Arcana gives an experience bonus that grows with rank. Carrying a Persona of the matching Arcana helps rank up. Maxing a link unlocks fusion of that Arcana's specific Personas — [Wikipedia: Persona 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persona_3)
- Wrong dialogue choices or dating several characters at once can reverse or break a link. A broken link blocks use of that Arcana's Personas until you reconcile — [Wikipedia: Persona 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persona_3)
- Each day has daytime and evening segments. Most activities use up time, and availability depends on weekday and time of day. Three social stats (Academics, Charm, Courage) gate some activities. Full-moon nights bring story boss battles — [Wikipedia: Persona 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persona_3)
- The team saw the social elements as successors to the series' demon-negotiation system, which "still factors into Persona Fusion" (Nich Maragos, 1UP). In Japan the links are called コミュ (community) — [Wikipedia: Persona 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persona_3)
- Reception: GameSpy called the links "almost universally fascinating". The epilogue "The Answer", which dropped social elements, was judged "less interesting" — [Wikipedia: Persona 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persona_3)
- Game Informer credits Hashino with introducing the Social Link system in P3, which gives relationship effects plus optional storylines — [Game Informer 2025](https://www.gameinformer.com/feature/2025/05/02/the-lost-metaphor-refantazio-cover-story-fantasy-fulfillment)
- P4 design (article narration): "The calendar was designed such that it would coincide with the pressure the protagonists were feeling." Late in the game the calendar shows a month at a time to signal that free time is running out. "The locations of the game as well as the game system were all designed with the story in mind." Social-link dialogue was a reason the game "barely fit onto a DVD." Many of the characters' personal problems were drawn from the staff's own or friends' high-school experiences — [Siliconera: Behind the Design of Persona 4](https://www.siliconera.com/behind-the-design-of-persona-4/)
- P5: Hashino (Persona Magazine 2016) wanted human relations to go "a step above" P3/P4 social links, asking whether communication can drive people who want to change things, and called the system one of the game's driving forces — [Destructoid](https://destructoid.com/persona-5-aims-for-human-relations-a-step-above-social-links); [Twinfinite](https://twinfinite.net/ps4/persona-5-director-talks-characters-dungeon-social-link-more/)
- Fire Emblem supports: compatible units earn support points when adjacent in battle, and enough points unlock support conversations at ranks C→B→A→S, with growing combat bonuses. Each unit can have only one S support. In Radiant Dawn, B doubles and A triples the C bonuses, based on fixed affinity — [StrategyWiki FE Awakening Supports](https://strategywiki.org/wiki/Fire_Emblem_Awakening/Supports); [GamerGuides FE Awakening](https://www.gamerguides.com/fire-emblem-awakening/intro-and-gameplay/gameplay/support-relationships) (fan or guide sources; numbers vary by game)
- Blazing Sword capped each unit at 5 support conversations in total and 3 with any one partner, which forces choices — [LP Archive FE7](https://lparchive.org/Fire-Emblem-Blazing-Sword-(by-Melth)/Update%2019)
- Counterexamples: Radiant Dawn let anyone pair with anyone and reset supports at any time, without a bonding-conversation arc. Fire Emblem Warriors gave conversations only at A+, and Siliconera argued this made supports feel "little practical" — [Siliconera FE Warriors](https://www.siliconera.com/fire-emblem-warriors-makes-supports-little-practical/); [LP Archive FE Radiant Dawn](https://lparchive.org/Fire-Emblem-Radiant-Dawn/Update%2010)

### Inferences
- **Make the emotional choice the powerful choice.** Persona's Arcana-to-fusion multiplier means "who do I spend tonight with?" is both a story choice and a power choice, so players don't feel they are wasting time on story. For volleyball, a teammate's bond rank could boost combo or tag training with her, or unlock a signature duo skill in matches.
- **Ranks are cheap story units.** A 10-rank link is 10 short scenes with a clear arc. Finite, numbered progress shows authored content in bite-sized pieces, which suits a solo writer: write a 3- to 5-beat arc per character.
- **Scarcity creates meaning.** A limited calendar (P3/P4) and conversation caps (FE7) force a choice, and choosing implies caring. When supports became unlimited or resettable (Radiant Dawn), the story arc vanished.
- **Relationships emerge from play actions** in FE (fighting side by side). That suits a volleyball match: a setter–spiker pair who play many rallies together could build bond points.
- **Reversal and breakage** (P3) add stakes but punish exploration. Later Persona entries reportedly softened this (not sourced here).

### Gaps
- Exact P3/P4 rank-up point rules, fusion bonus percentages, and P5 Confidant gameplay perks (e.g., extra actions, negotiation abilities) were not retrieved: the Megami Tensei fandom wiki returned HTTP 402.
- No Hashino quote explaining why relationships were tied to fusion power was found. The Source Gaming translation, page 2, returned 404.

## 5. Designer theory: storylets, quality-based narrative, and how small teams produce lots of character content

### Takeaway
The storylet or quality-based model (Failbetter, Emily Short) is the formal version of what Power Pro's event deck does in practice. It uses small self-contained content units with prerequisites on numeric "qualities" and effects that change those qualities. Content is "atomic, robust, and recombinable", can be added later with few side effects, and avoids combinatorial branching. The costs are bookkeeping, thin feel until there are enough units, and no built-in dramatic arc.

### Cited Findings
- QBN is Failbetter's term for "interactive narratives structured around storylets unlocked by qualities". Qualities are numeric variables tracking anything from inventory to skills to story progress. A storylet is a paragraph or two ending in a choice — [Emily Short, "Beyond Branching" (2016)](https://emshort.blog/2016/04/12/beyond-branching-quality-based-and-salience-based-narrative-structures/)
- Strengths: "it's possible to drop in modular elements of new content alongside old content". Storylets can gate later content (e.g., an accusation unlocks after 3 pieces of evidence) and call back to earlier ones. Weaknesses: "the content tends to be uninteresting until there are a fair number of storylets in the database", large catalogs overwhelm players, and progression tracking is "a lot of bookkeeping" — [Emily Short 2016](https://emshort.blog/2016/04/12/beyond-branching-quality-based-and-salience-based-narrative-structures/)
- Salience-based narrative picks the best-fitting content for the current world state (e.g., the most specific matching line wins, as in Left 4 Dead or Firewatch dialogue). It allows broad defaults plus specific overrides, but it can pick poorly paced content because it lacks dramatic-arc awareness, and weight tuning is trial and error. Short suggests random playthroughs and visualization as remedies — [Emily Short 2016](https://emshort.blog/2016/04/12/beyond-branching-quality-based-and-salience-based-narrative-structures/)
- Waypoint narrative (Short's *Glass*): the engine steers toward authored beats, "constantly trying to heal the story" — [Emily Short 2016](https://emshort.blog/2016/04/12/beyond-branching-quality-based-and-salience-based-narrative-structures/)
- A storylet has three parts: content, prerequisites, and effects. Short calls them "simple, atomic, robust, and recombinable" and "a good bet for a game that's meant to last for years" (DLC, live content, mods) — [Emily Short, "Storylets: You Want Them" (2019)](https://emshort.blog/2019/11/29/storylets-you-want-them/)
- A progress quality plus an optional "menace" quality (how much trouble you're in) lets storylets reproduce gauntlet, branch-and-bottleneck, sorting-hat and loop-and-grow structures. Storylets can be gated by gameplay levels, resources, or grinds, as in Lily's Garden, where match-3 play unlocks story — [Emily Short 2019](https://emshort.blog/2019/11/29/storylets-you-want-them/)
- Pitfall: time caves (content that depends on the exact route taken) are "possibly the most annoying type of work to build with storylets". Short recommends embedding small branching segments within the larger storylet structure. Storylets work like paper-prototype index cards and decks, but the analogy "fades the more internal complexity the content has" — [Emily Short 2019](https://emshort.blog/2019/11/29/storylets-you-want-them/)
- Early Fallen London used grinds to gate content. Short says this was "a common structure in the early days" and the system has since moved on — [Emily Short 2019](https://emshort.blog/2019/11/29/storylets-you-want-them/)
- Short joined Failbetter as Creative Director in January 2020 — [Emily Short, "Joining Failbetter"](https://emshort.blog/2020/01/02/joining-failbetter/)

### Inferences
- **Map the Power Pro event deck onto storylets.** Each card owns a small pool of storylets with prerequisites (bond ≥ N, week range, stat ≥ X, a teammate also in deck) and effects (stats, bond, mood, flags). This keeps content additive: a new card adds a new pool and never rewrites old ones, which fits a solo developer shipping characters over time.
- **Bottleneck to authored climaxes.** Use a fixed run spine (practice weeks → tournament → final) as waypoints, and let storylets fill the gaps. This answers salience's "no dramatic arc" weakness the same way Power Pro and Tokimeki do.
- **"Menace" equals fatigue or injury risk.** A danger quality gives storylets something to react to (a worried teammate scene at high fatigue) and makes failure tell a story.
- **Simulate before shipping.** Short's advice on random playthroughs matches the project's harness-first culture. A Monte-Carlo run of the event selector can check that each character's arc completes at a reasonable rate within about 10-minute runs.
- **Minimum content threshold.** Storylet systems feel empty until they hold enough units, so a launch needs a dense pool for a few characters rather than a thin pool for many.

### Gaps
- No GDC Vault talk transcript (e.g., Failbetter's or Short's GDC narrative talks) was retrieved. Their content is not asserted here.

## 6. Player complaints and pitfalls

### Takeaway
The recurring failures are repetition of the core loop (Princess Maker's train-rest cycle), luck-dependence and gacha power gaps that make outcomes feel out of your hands (Power Pro), guide-dependence created by hidden thresholds and fine timing rules (Tokimeki bombs, Persona's optimal schedules), and systems that shed their story when they become pure optimization (FE Warriors / Radiant Dawn supports).

### Cited Findings
- PM2's training-and-rest cycle was criticized as repetitive, and Akai's sequel design responded with more reactions, events, and multiple paths — [Wikipedia: PM2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princess_Maker_2)
- Power Pro-style Success: event timing has a "gambling" quality and "in the end real luck is needed". SR cards are so strong that PR/R cards are hard to raise — [Inside Games 2026](https://www.inside-games.jp/article/2026/05/04/180880.html)
- Optimal-play advice in Power Pro App coverage tends to strip choice: stick to one girlfriend (no two-timing) and fill her super-ability's prerequisites with your other event cards. Turn down risky surgery when a run is going well — [famitsu Pawapro 2016 special](https://www.famitsu.com/matome/pawapro2016/success03.html) (via search summary); [4Gamer 2018](https://www.4gamer.net/games/278/G027881/20180807001)
- Tokimeki's bomb chain can tank your reputation with everyone, and guides advise moderate contact with all girls — [Game Watch](https://game.watch.impress.co.jp/docs/kikaku/1593642.html)
- FE Warriors limited support conversations to A+ only, which critics said made supports feel impractical. Radiant Dawn's free, resettable pairing removed the bonding arc — [Siliconera](https://www.siliconera.com/fire-emblem-warriors-makes-supports-little-practical/); [LP Archive](https://lparchive.org/Fire-Emblem-Radiant-Dawn/Update%2010)
- P3's epilogue without social elements was judged less interesting, which shows the relationships were carrying engagement — [Wikipedia: Persona 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persona_3)

### Inferences
- **When the optimal path is fixed, emotion dies.** If one girlfriend or one card set is always correct, the "choice" becomes a guide lookup. Countermeasures: make several characters viable with different trade-offs (Tokimeki's stat-keyed heroines), or rotate scenario modifiers so the best deck changes.
- **Hidden thresholds breed wikis.** Show bond rank and next-event hints in the UI (Persona shows rank, Power Pro shows a rating gauge) so players plan instead of searching.
- **Luck should create stories, not void effort.** Put randomness in which event appears, not in whether hard-earned progress is erased. Use soft failure (Power Pro's red abilities) rather than hard deletion.
- **Repetition needs reaction density.** Over many 10-minute runs, short varied reaction lines and character callbacks (Fallen London-style "you did X last time") do more than new mechanics.
- **The gacha power gap can split story from strength.** If a beloved low-rarity character is useless, attachment and optimization fight each other. Consider letting bond progress or story completion lift a card's ceiling.

### Gaps
- No systematic player-survey or review-aggregate data on guide-dependence or min-maxing in these titles was found. The items above are drawn from individual reviews and guides.
- Dated info: Power Pro App scenario counts and deck rules are from 2018 coverage, and the series may have changed since. The Persona 5 statements date from 2016.
